A kitchen holds more water sources in one room than any other space in a home. The sink has two supply lines and a drain, the dishwasher sits in its own alcove, and the refrigerator may have a water line. Add cabinets, a countertop seam and a floor laid over a subfloor, and a small leak has plenty of places to hide.

This page covers the kitchen as a room, from the sink base to the ceiling below an upstairs kitchen. If you can see water moving, shut off the supply and call the 24/7 line.

How Kitchen Water Damage Starts in College Park Homes

Most kitchen leaks begin under the sink. Supply lines age at the fittings, a slip nut on the drain loosens, or a disposal flange seeps. The water runs down the back of the cabinet and into the toe kick before anyone sees it. Because closed doors hide the leak, it can go on for weeks.

The second common start is the countertop edge. Water that gets past a sink rim or a backsplash seam soaks the cabinet top and the particleboard below it. Dishwasher alcoves and refrigerator lines add a third path into the floor.

Kitchen Safety First: Water, Power and Gas

  1. Close the small angle stops under the sink, one on each supply line, by turning them clockwise. If they do not hold, close the main water shut-off for the home.
  2. Mind the electricity. Disposals, dishwashers and countertop outlets share this room with water. Turn off the circuit at the panel only if you can reach it standing on a dry floor, and otherwise call 911 or the electric utility.
  3. If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not flip switches on the way out.
  4. Keep out from under a ceiling that sags below an upstairs kitchen.
  5. Photograph the cabinet base, the floor and any stains before you wipe anything.
  6. Move food and small items out of wet lower cabinets, and prop the doors open.

What Dries in Place and What Comes Out

Kitchen drying is a sorting job. Solid wood cabinets, tile and sealed stone often dry in place. Particleboard, damp insulation and soft drywall often do not. The technician reads moisture in every layer and marks what stays.

MaterialOften dried in placeOften removed
Cabinet basesSolid wood or plywood that dried fastSwollen particleboard and laminate-faced bases
Toe kicksTaken off, dried and reinstalledSwollen or musty kick boards
Vinyl and laminate flooringRarely, since water runs under the seamsCut and lifted in the wet zone
TileOften, with drying from below and through the groutLoose tile over a wet mortar bed
SubfloorPlywood that reads dry after dryingSoft or delaminating panels
Drywall below the counterFirm and intact after dryingSoft, stained or crumbling sections

On-site readings decide each item.

Equipment follows the sort. After surface water is extracted, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while a thermal camera and moisture meter track the wet layers. Toe kicks come off so air reaches the cabinet base, and small holes behind the base let dry air into the wall cavity. Drying mats pull moisture out of the subfloor, and HEPA air scrubbers run when anything is cut out.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference for this work, and it is why drying continues until readings return to normal rather than until a floor feels dry. Once the kitchen is dry, the rebuild is covered under water damage repair, which replaces drywall, flooring and finishes.

Upstairs Kitchens and the Ceiling Below

In a two-story home or a stacked apartment, a kitchen leak often appears as a stain on the ceiling below. Water follows joists and wiring holes, so the stain can sit several feet from the source. Do not poke it.

We dry the joist bay from below through small openings and check the kitchen floor above at the same visit. The drying method matches the one on our water mitigation page, applied to a cavity instead of an open room.

Kitchen Leaks and Your Insurance

A sudden supply failure is often treated differently from a drip that went on for months. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, and long-term seepage often is not. Policies commonly pay for the resulting damage rather than the failed part. Check your policy and ask your insurer, who decides coverage.

If you rent, tell the landlord in writing first, because cabinets and flooring in a rental usually belong to the owner. Your belongings may fall under your own renters policy. We also dry kitchens in Beltsville and Hyattsville.

Kitchen Leak Mistakes and Prevention

  • Do not paint or caulk over a stain. The water source and the wet layers are still there.
  • Do not close the cabinet doors on a wet sink base. Airflow is the first step in drying it.
  • Do not use a heat gun or space heater on wet cabinets. Fast heat can warp them.
  • Check the supply lines under the sink monthly with a dry paper towel, and replace lines with rust or bulges.
  • Do not put stored items back before the base reads dry.
