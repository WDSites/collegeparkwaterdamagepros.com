Water Damage Restoration in Old Town, College Park, MD

Old Town's older houses, plaster walls and rental buildings dry differently from new construction. Here is what that means for tenants, landlords and owners.

Old Town is a residential and mixed-use neighborhood in College Park, across Route 1 from downtown and the university campus, according to the University of Maryland's Office of Community Engagement. The City lists an Old Town Civic Association and describes the area as a locally designated historic district. The Maryland Historical Trust also lists the Old Town College Park Historic District on the National Register, with a December 2012 listing date.

People call about water here for ordinary reasons. A washer hose lets go in an upstairs unit, a roof leaks above a bedroom, or a pipe fails in an empty house over a break. What makes the work different is who is involved and what the buildings are made of.

A Mixed Housing Stock

The Trust's listing counts 222 resources in the district, 148 of them contributing and 60 not. It names single-family houses in American Foursquare, Cape Cod, bungalow, ranch and split-foyer forms, along with garden apartment complexes, fraternity and sorority houses, a church and a fire department building. The district lies between U.S. Route 1 and the old B&O railroad line. We do not use any street-by-street boundary, because published descriptions disagree.

The university's housing page describes a mix of one-unit detached homes and small to medium multi-unit buildings, with roughly 22 percent owner-occupied and 78 percent renter-occupied. Those figures are undated and approximate. They tell us who is likely to call, not how often water damage happens. We have no data on damage frequency for Old Town.

Tenants and landlords have different obligations under their lease and Maryland law. We do not give legal advice, so read your lease and ask the right agency or attorney.

Group rentals add a few wrinkles. Utilities may be in one name, nobody may be home for a week, and several tenants may each hold separate belongings. Tell us who has keys and who needs to be present.

Plaster, Hardwood and Old Framing

Many older houses have plaster over lath rather than drywall, and wood floors laid over board subfloors. Plaster holds water and dries slowly, so a crew monitors it longer and cuts only where drying fails.

Hardwood can cup or crack when wet, so drying starts quickly with mats or panels that pull moisture from the boards. We do not know whether a given house has plaster, a basement or a crawl space until someone looks. Age alone does not tell us.

For ceilings and walls that need repair after drying, see water damage repair. When the cause is a supply line, our burst pipe water damage page explains the shutoff and the cleanup.

Safety Steps Before Anyone Starts Cleaning

Check for electrical hazards

If water is near outlets, a panel or lights, keep out and keep others out. Do not operate breakers while standing in water.

Stop the source if you can

Close a fixture valve or the main shutoff when it is dry and reachable. If you cannot reach it, call the landlord or a plumber.

Do not use fans on dirty water

Fans and household vacuums are not safe for sewage or contaminated water.

Photograph and list

Photograph the room before moving anything and list affected items. Tenants should send the landlord that record in writing.

Historic District Rules

The City says work on a home or lot in the Old Town historic district follows the district's design guidelines. Water cleanup inside is rarely affected, but exterior repairs after a roof or siding leak may be. Before changing exterior features, ask the City's Planning and Community Development department what applies. We tell customers when that question comes up.

Extraction and Drying

A crew removes standing water with extractors, then maps moisture in floors and walls with meters and a thermal camera. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach a dry standard. Wet insulation and damaged board are removed where drying will not work. Water mitigation describes the stages, and ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference for the method.

Insurance and Documentation

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the loss. Coverage depends on the cause and the policy, and a landlord's policy and a tenant's renter policy cover different things. Read the policy and ask the insurer. We cannot promise payment or approval.
