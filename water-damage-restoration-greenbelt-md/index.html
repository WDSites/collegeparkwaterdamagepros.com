Water damage restoration in Greenbelt starts with stormwater. The City of Greenbelt's Community Flood Resiliency Plan, dated April 2026, says intense, short-duration summer storms overwhelm the local drainage system and cause localized flooding in low-lying areas. Our 24/7 emergency line serves Greenbelt, about three miles northeast of College Park, and this page explains what that history means for you.

The Stormwater Problem Greenbelt Has Documented

The plan describes a July 19, 2025 storm that dropped about 2.72 inches of rain in two hours and caused extensive flooding, followed by a July 31 storm of about 1.83 inches over seven hours.

An earlier event on August 10, 2022 brought rain at up to three inches per hour, according to the National Weather Service as reported by the Greenbelt News Review. The mayor said at the time that the rainfall was more than the stormwater system could handle.

The plan names specific places. Inadequate storm drain inlet capacity was the likely primary issue at Lakeside Drive and Westway in July 2025. Pipes under parking lots near Crescent Road and Centerway, close to Roosevelt Center, lack capacity, and the plan cites potentially dangerous basement apartment flooding in that area. If you live or work there, take photos of any water and call us for extraction.

When Water Is Spreading in Greenbelt

Basement and ground-floor flooding is the emergency pattern here. Water that rises quickly can reach outlets and panels before you notice it.

A Planned Town With Its Own Housing Types

Greenbelt was the first of three New Deal Greenbelt Towns. Construction began on January 13, 1936, and the first tenants moved in on September 30, 1937. The original core, Old Greenbelt, is the Greenbelt Historic District, listed on the National Register in 1980 and designated a National Historic Landmark District in 1997.

Greenbelt Homes, Inc., a cooperative of 1,600 homes, built its first homes in 1937, and its members own the co-op together and share its costs. If you live in a co-op home, check your cooperative's rules on who handles repairs and insurance, and tell the co-op's maintenance staff as well as your insurer. Photos help both.

Indian Creek, Greenbelt Lake and What the Plan Says About Flood Risk

Riverine flooding is a separate matter from street flooding. The plan models 100-year and 500-year floods along Indian Creek, and says a 100-year flood would affect five buildings near the Franklin Park area while a 500-year flood would affect twelve. The Greenbelt Lake forebay and nearby athletic fields are also expected to flood, and the plan treats that as more acceptable than building flooding.

The plan also notes that the city could encourage residents to keep storm drains clear, disconnect rooftop downspouts to pervious areas and add rain gardens. These are practical steps for a Greenbelt lot, and they do not replace flood insurance. Check the FEMA flood map for your address.
