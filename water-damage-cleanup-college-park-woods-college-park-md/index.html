What Cleanup Usually Involves in a House

Most house calls involve a supply line, a dishwasher or washer hose, a water heater or a roof leak. Each has its own page: appliance leak water damage, kitchen water damage and carpet water damage cover the rooms and materials we see most often.

  • Pumps and extractors remove standing water and soaked carpet pad.
  • Moisture meters show which framing, subfloor and drywall are wet.
  • Air movers and dehumidifiers are set to a drying plan.
  • Damaged materials that cannot dry are cut out and bagged.

Safety First: The First Hour

Keep clear of unsafe water

Stay out of water that touches outlets, cords or the panel. Do not touch breakers or electrical equipment while standing in water.

Shut the water off

Close the main shutoff, or the supply valve at a washer or sink, only if you can reach it safely.

Know when it is sewage

If water comes up from a drain, keep people and pets away and call WSSC Water. Do not use household fans or a household vacuum on it.

Document it

Take photos and video before anything is moved. Note the time you found the water.

Drying and Insurance Records

After extraction, drying is the long part. Air movers and dehumidifiers run while meters track the moisture in each material. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference for how that work is planned. Wet carpet may dry in place if the water was clean and the response was quick, and contaminated water changes that. See water mitigation for more.

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help document the loss for an insurer. Coverage depends on the cause and the policy, so ask your insurer before you agree to work. We do not decide coverage and cannot promise approval or payment.
