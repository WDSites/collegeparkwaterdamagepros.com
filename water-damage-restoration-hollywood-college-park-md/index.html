Water Damage Scenarios That Fit a Detached Home

We are not claiming any one kind of damage is common in Hollywood, because no source we found says so. These are the scenarios that apply to almost any detached house, and they are the ones people describe when they call.

  • A failed hose or fitting on a washer, dishwasher or ice maker, covered on our appliance leak water damage page
  • A water heater leak at the tank, valve or fittings, covered on water heater leak cleanup
  • A slow plumbing leak inside a wall or under a floor that shows up as a stain or a soft spot
  • Heavy rain that finds a way in at a door, window or foundation edge
  • A sewer or drain backup, where the water should be treated as contaminated

First-Response Safety

Safety comes before cleanup. Keep people and pets out of any water that may be contaminated or that touches electrical outlets, cords or appliances. Do not touch the breaker panel, a switch or any electrical equipment while you are standing in water. If the water is clean and coming from a pipe, shut it off at the fixture valve or the main, but only if you can reach it without wading.

Stop the source

Close the fixture valve or the main water shutoff when it is dry and reachable.

Stay out of risky water

If a basement, laundry room or hall has standing water near outlets, leave it alone and call.

Photograph from a safe spot

Take wide and close photos of the damage and the source before anything is moved.

Protect what is dry

Lift small items, rugs and electronics off wet floors only where it is safe to step.

Extraction and Drying

The first job is mapping the wet area. Readings from a moisture meter and images from a thermal camera reveal damp drywall, insulation and subfloor that look normal on the surface. An extractor takes up any standing water. Wet carpet pad and soaked drywall near the floor are often cut out so the cavity can dry.

Fans move air across the wet surfaces while dehumidifiers remove the moisture those surfaces give off. A crew comes back to check readings until the materials are dry. For reference, the drying method follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 industry standard. Our water mitigation page explains the stages, and the wall and drywall repair page covers what happens once a wall is dry.
