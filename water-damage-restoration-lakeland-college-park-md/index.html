Lakeland is one of the older named communities in College Park, and most of its homes today are townhouses and multifamily buildings. That changes how water damage plays out. A leak in one unit can wet a ceiling below, a shared wall beside it or a hallway outside, and the people who need to act include the tenant, the property manager and the owner.

Where Lakeland Is

The Lakeland Civic Association describes the neighborhood as running roughly from Berwyn House Road on the north to Lakeland Road and Pierce Avenue on the south, and from 54th Street on the east to Baltimore Avenue on the west.

That is the association's description as reported by local press, and it is not an official boundary. The City of College Park's own district text treats the portion west of the CSX tracks as part of District Two.

The City lists a Lakeland civic association and a neighborhood watch, and council minutes from March 12, 2024 record a Lakeland Park ribbon cutting. If your address is in or near these streets, this page is written for you.

Housing That Is Mostly Rented

The University of Maryland's off-campus housing office describes Lakeland as predominantly high-density multifamily housing and townhouses, with about 14 percent owner-occupied and 86 percent renter-occupied. That page is undated, so the figures are an approximation. The renters it lists include families, seniors and students.

That mix matters for a restoration call. Renters often cannot decide what comes out of a wall, and owners may not be in the building when water starts moving. Clear communication between the tenant and the property manager shortens the time water sits in materials.

First-Response Safety

Safety comes first, and it is the same in an apartment as in a house. Keep people and pets away from water that may be contaminated or that is near outlets, cords or appliances. Do not touch a breaker panel or any electrical equipment while standing in water. Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the unit's main only if it is dry and easy to reach.

Stop the source

Close the valve to the leaking fixture or appliance when it is safe to reach.

Tell the manager

Call the property manager or landlord and say which unit and room is wet, and which units may be affected.

Photograph first

Take wide and close photos of the ceiling, walls and floor, including any unit below, before anything moves.

Call for help

For gas or electrical danger, call 911. For sewage coming through a drain, WSSC Water's 24-hour line is 301-206-4002.

Drying a Shared Building

Drying in a multi-unit building starts with a moisture map, because water travels in ceilings and walls farther than the stain suggests. Extractors remove standing water, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rooms, and a thermal camera helps find wet material behind surfaces. Our water mitigation page explains the stages.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 industry standard is the reference point for this work. In a rental, the scope usually has to be agreed with the owner or manager as well as the tenant, so we write down what was wet, what was removed and what was dried.

Flood History and What Is Verified

The Lakeland Community Heritage Project's history page says the original neighborhood sat on low, wet land and that nearly all of it was within a 100-year flooding zone. It gives no date, and it describes the neighborhood before redevelopment, so it is not a current FEMA finding. The Prince George's County flood history page also lists Lakeland among the hardest-hit areas in Tropical Storm Agnes in June 1972.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your own address. We found no documented recent flood or drainage event for Lakeland.

Records for Tenants and Managers

Photos, dated notes and moisture readings help describe what happened and when. Tenants should read their lease and their renter's insurance policy, and managers should check the building's policy. Whether any loss is covered depends on the cause of the damage and the policy terms, and we cannot promise payment or approval.
