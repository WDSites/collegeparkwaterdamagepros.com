What Water Damage Looks Like Across the Area

The causes repeat from town to town. Older houses have older supply lines and drain pipes, and finished basements hide leaks until a smell or a stain shows up. Rental housing near campus sees heavy appliance and plumbing use, and commercial buildings along Route 1 share walls and roofs, so one leak can reach neighboring units. Storm runoff adds a second pattern, because several creeks and drainage paths cross this part of Prince George's County.

Because of that, the first call usually settles two things. One is whether the water is clean, gray or contaminated, and the other is whether it is still coming in. Those answers decide the first hour of work, whichever town the job is in.

Planning Ahead for Storm Season

Most storm-related calls come after heavy rain, when water enters through basement windows, foundation cracks or backed-up drains. A few habits lower the risk. Keep gutters and downspouts clear, direct them away from the foundation, and test a sump pump before the wet months if you have one. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer about flood and sewer backup coverage, which are often separate from a standard policy.

If water does get in, the same rules apply everywhere: stay out of wet areas with live electricity, take photos before moving anything, and call the 24/7 line.
