Water damage cleanup in College Park is the stage after the leak is stopped and the standing water is out. It covers removing materials that cannot be dried, cleaning and sanitizing what remains, and clearing odors so the building can be dried and rebuilt. It sits between emergency water removal and water damage repair.

What Gets Removed and What Gets Saved

The deciding factors are the kind of water and how long materials stayed wet. Clean water that is caught the same day leaves far more salvageable material than contaminated water that sits for days. A technician checks each material with a moisture meter and does not rely on appearance alone.

Sanitizing and Deodorizing After the Water Stops

Once wet materials are out, hard surfaces and framing are cleaned with detergent and water and then dried completely. The EPA recommends scrubbing mold and grime off hard surfaces with detergent and water, and says that biocides such as chlorine bleach are not recommended as a routine part of mold cleanup. Technicians use antimicrobial products where the water category calls for them, and follow the label.

Odor is a sign that moisture or contamination is still present. Air scrubbers and thorough drying clear most smells. Deodorizing without drying only hides the problem, and the smell returns the first humid week.

What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away

FEMA advises documenting damage with photos and video first, then discarding flooded items that pose a health risk, such as food, pillows, cushions and clothing. It also advises keeping receipts and checking with your insurer before you sign a cleanup or remediation agreement. Samples of damaged carpet or fabric can help show the type and quality of the material.

  1. Take photos and video of every wet room and item before moving anything.
  2. Set aside items that are clean and can be dried, and move them to a dry room.
  3. Discard food, porous soft goods and anything that touched sewage or flood water after documenting it.
  4. Keep receipts for cleanup, storage and replacement, and write down the date and time of each step.

If the Water Was Contaminated

Sewage, creek water and long-standing water are Category 3 under the S500 standard and need a different plan. Porous materials that touched this water are usually removed, not cleaned. The people doing the work wear protective gear, and the area is sealed off from living spaces while it is cleaned.

Stay out of contaminated water

Do not wade into sewage or flood water, and do not run fans or vacuums in it. Keep children and pets out. If electrical equipment is under or near the water, call the electric utility or 911 before anything else.

For a sewer backup in a WSSC Water service area, the utility's 24-hour sewer backup line is 301-206-4002. Call before you clean so the situation can be documented.
