Sewage backup cleanup is the removal of contaminated water and the materials it soaked, followed by cleaning, sanitizing and drying. It is separate from a clean supply-line leak, because the water is treated as grossly contaminated from the moment it arrives.

How a Sewage Backup Happens in College Park Homes

Wastewater leaves a house through a lateral, the pipe that runs from the building to the sewer main under the street. A backup happens when something stops that flow. The blockage can sit in the lateral on your property, or further along in the main. Which one it is decides who has to clear it.

The lowest drain in the house gives way first, because that is where the water finds the easiest exit. In a College Park basement that is usually a floor drain, a laundry standpipe, a basement shower or a tub. Upstairs fixtures stay quiet while the lowest one overflows.

Signs the Water Came From a Drain, Not a Pipe

A supply leak is clear water that stops when you shut a valve. A drain backup is cloudy or dark, it keeps rising or returns after you stop using water, and it carries a strong smell. Toilets that bubble when you run the washer, and floor drains that gurgle, point to a blocked lateral.

Look for a ring on a basement wall that shows the high point, wet carpet near a drain, and paper or debris left on the floor after the level drops. Tell us about each of these when you call, because they change the plan.

First-Hour Safety Steps for a Sewage Backup

Treat everything the water touched as contaminated. Do not wade through it in bare feet, and do not use a household vacuum on it. The steps below protect people and slow the spread while help is on the way.

  • Keep people and pets out of the wet area and out of rooms that connect to it.
  • Stop using sinks, tubs, showers, toilets and the washer so no more water enters the line.
  • If water has reached outlets, appliances or the panel, get out of the room and phone the electric utility or 911.
  • Gas odor means you go outside first and dial 911 from there.
  • Call WSSC Water's 24-hour sewer backup line before you clean so the event is on record.
  • Take photos and video of the water, the drains and every affected item before anything moves.

How We Clean Up After a Backup

The crew arrives in protective gear and sets up containment before anything is moved. Plastic barriers and a negative air machine with HEPA filtration keep dust and odor from traveling into rooms that stayed clean. Foot traffic is routed through one path.

  1. Pump or extract the standing water with a truck-mounted or portable extractor and dispose of it correctly.
  2. Remove porous materials the water touched, such as carpet, pad, insulation and soaked drywall, and bag them on site.
  3. Wash hard surfaces and framing with detergent, then apply a sanitizer suited to the surface.
  4. Dry the structure with air movers and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers, checking moisture meters until readings match dry reference materials.
  5. Run air scrubbers to clear airborne dust and odor before the containment comes down.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference that classes sewage and flood water as Category 3. That class is why carpet and pad are removed instead of cleaned. A thermal camera helps find wet wall cavities that look dry from the surface.

What Changes the Cost of Sewage Cleanup

Price follows the size of the affected area, how long the water sat, and how many materials must be removed. Finished basements with carpet, pad and drywall cost more to restore than bare concrete with a floor drain. Access, drying time and the number of rooms the water crossed also matter.

Insurance and Sewage Backups

Many homeowners policies treat drain and sewer backup as a separate coverage, often sold as an endorsement. Whether yours includes it depends on the wording, so check your policy and ask your insurer before you assume either way. Sudden backups and gradual seepage are usually handled differently.

Your insurer will want photos, a description of what happened, and receipts. We keep moisture readings, dated photos and a list of removed materials. That record helps whoever reviews the claim, whether it is your insurer or the utility.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Backup

The most common mistake is cleaning it yourself before anyone has documented it. Mopping and bagging carpet may feel responsible, but it spreads contamination and destroys the evidence a claim needs.

  • Do not run a wet vac or household vacuum on the water.
  • Do not flush or run water to see whether the drain has cleared.
  • Do not mix cleaning chemicals.
  • Do not move soaked boxes and furniture into clean rooms.
  • Do not wait for the smell to fade. The materials still need to come out and dry.
