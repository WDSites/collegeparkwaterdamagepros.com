Where Beltsville Sits: Streets, Neighborhoods and the Research Center

South Beltsville was laid out as a street grid by the Beltsville Land Improvement Company starting in 1891, and Vansville is a community inside the CDP boundary. The Henry A. Wallace Beltsville Agricultural Research Center, a USDA facility that covers about 14,600 acres, is a large landmark. Just to the south, North College Park is the neighboring community, and it is covered through the College Park pages.

Because Beltsville is a census-designated place and not an incorporated city, it has no city government of its own. Permit questions for work after water damage go to Prince George's County, and utilities come from WSSC Water.

When Water Is Spreading in Beltsville

Treat spreading water as an emergency, even if it looks small. The steps below buy time while you reach the line.

If Water Is Still Spreading

Shut off the supply

Turn off the main valve or the one at the leaking fixture. A leaking washing machine or water heater has its own shutoff.

Mind the electricity

Cut power to the wet area only from a dry panel you can reach. Otherwise leave it and call the utility or 911.

Raise what is low

Move rugs, electronics and cardboard boxes off the floor, because cardboard wicks water upward.

Capture the damage

Take wide and close photos, then record a short walk-through video. Write down the time you found it.

Summer Storms, Winter Freezes and Spring Humidity

Summer thunderstorms are the usual cause of basement and roof leaks, since heavy rain can arrive faster than drains can move it. In winter, an unheated closet or crawl space is where a supply line splits. In spring and early fall, humid air meets cool surfaces and a damp basement stays damp.

Each season calls for a different first step. After a storm, check ceilings under the roof and basement corners. After a freeze, find the shutoff before looking for the break. In humid months, run a dehumidifier and watch for musty smells, because the EPA advises keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent.

Housing Eras: Streetcar Subdivisions, 1930s Cottages and Postwar Neighborhoods

An electric streetcar line along what is now Rhode Island Avenue spurred subdivisions around Beltsville, and early houses were Victorian and Colonial Revival, marketed to professionals leaving Washington. Modest side-gable homes followed in the 1930s and 1940s, and intensive development came after World War II. That gives Beltsville a mix of plaster-and-hardwood houses and postwar structures.

Older houses tend to hold water longer in plaster and subfloor, while postwar homes more often have finished basements where a sump or sewer problem causes the loss. Both end up with water damage repair after drying. Our crews adjust to each house type once they see it.
