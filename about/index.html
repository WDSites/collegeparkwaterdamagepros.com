How We Work

Every job follows the same order. A technician finds the source of the water, takes moisture readings, explains the scope and the price, and only then begins. Extraction and drying come first. Cleaning, repairs and, when needed, mold work follow.

Photos, moisture readings and written notes are kept for your insurer. We document the loss. Your insurer decides coverage, so we tell you to check your policy and ask your agent.

Industry Standards We Point To

Water damage restoration practice is published in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and mold remediation practice is published in ANSI/IICRC S520. These are the industry's published references, and it is fair to ask any restoration company, including us, whether its crews work to them. We name the standards because they are the language of the trade.

What We Do Not Do

  • We do not waterproof basements or repair foundations. If water keeps coming in from outside, you need a waterproofing or foundation specialist.
  • We do not make health claims or diagnose illness from mold or water exposure. Health questions belong to a physician.
  • We do not guarantee insurance coverage, and we do not tell you what a claim will pay.
  • We do not quote exact prices without seeing the damage.

What a First Visit Looks Like

A technician arrives, checks for safety hazards and finds where the water came from. Moisture meters and, where useful, thermal imaging show how far the water has traveled behind walls and under floors. The technician then walks through what is wet, what can be dried in place and what has to come out, and explains the price before any work starts.

You decide what happens next. If you want time to call your insurer or compare options, that is a reasonable thing to do. Water does not wait, though, so standing water and soaked materials usually come first.

How We Handle Records and Documentation

Water losses turn into insurance conversations quickly, so the paperwork matters as much as the pumps. Before work begins we photograph the affected rooms, record moisture readings at set points and note the source of the water. Readings are taken again during drying so that the end of the job is measured rather than guessed.

That record belongs to you. If your insurer asks for it, we provide it. If you are not filing a claim, it still helps when you sell the house or hire a contractor to rebuild. Ask for it at the end of the job.

Plain Answers on Price

No one can price a water loss over the phone, and we do not try. What we can tell you is what drives the number. Square footage affected, the category of water, how long materials sat wet and whether anything has to be torn out all change it. After the first visit you get a scope and a price before work starts.

What to Do Before Help Arrives

Turn off the water source if you can reach it safely, stay out of rooms where water touches electrical equipment, and take photos before moving anything. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
