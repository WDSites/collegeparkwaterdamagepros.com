Water problems come in stages, and each stage has its own trade name. Knowing which stage you are in helps you ask for the right help, and it helps you understand a scope of work when someone explains it. The six services below are listed in the order most people need them, and each has its own page with more detail.

How the Services Fit Together

Most jobs run in one order. The first step is stopping the source and removing standing water. Then the structure is dried, wet materials are cleaned out, and repairs follow. Mold remediation is added only when mold has formed, usually because drying was delayed.

That order matters because each step depends on the one before. New drywall hung over a damp wall invites mold. Cleanup done before the water is stopped has to be repeated. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, which is why the first services on the list are the urgent ones.

What Each Service Does Not Cover

Restoration is about the building after water gets in. It does not fix what lets water in. A failing foundation, a bad grade or a roof that sheds water onto a wall needs a different trade, and we say so when we see it. Plumbing repair, roofing and waterproofing are separate trades, and a restoration crew will tell you when one of them is needed before the dried structure is rebuilt.

Insurance is the other limit. We document the loss with photos, moisture readings and notes, but coverage is your insurer's decision. Check your policy and ask your agent what is covered, especially for flood and sewer backup, which many policies treat separately.

Reference Standards for Every Service

Each service on this page maps to published practice in ANSI/IICRC S500 for water and S520 for mold. The standards are the common language of the trade.
